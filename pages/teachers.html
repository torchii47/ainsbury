For Teachers

Free resources for your classes, from a T Level Digital specialist who has previously taught GCSE Computer Science. There are no accounts to set up and no student data to manage.

  • T Level Digital Software Development core guide: Pearson specification, CA1–CA8, the ESP and two mock papers.
  • OCR GCSE Computer Science guide: J277 topics 1.1–2.5, practical programming and two mock papers.

Both are independent resources, not produced or endorsed by Pearson, OCR or JCQ. You're welcome to use them with your students and print them, as long as you don't charge for them. See the terms of use.

Setting a mock

_□×
  • On paper: open the guide's Print page and choose Print paper. You get the questions with ruled answer lines and no mark schemes.
  • On screen: students open the mock and start the timer. Mark schemes stay locked until they press I've finished: show mark schemes.
  • Mark schemes: to print one, open the mock on your computer and press I've finished: show mark schemes, then use Print mark scheme.

Seeing how students are doing

_□×

Student progress is saved in their own browser, so you can't see it automatically and it isn't sent to this site. Students can share it with you themselves: on the guide's home page, backup_progress.exe makes a progress summary to paste into a message or your own form.

On shared computers, check whether browser data survives logging out. If it doesn't, ask students to download a progress file at the end of each session.

Things to check first

_□×
  • Your network: the Run buttons download Python from jsDelivr the first time, so check your school or college filter allows it.
  • The specification: always check content against the current specification.
  • Exam dates: the dates shown may be provisional.
  • Mock papers: these are practice papers, not real exam papers, and shouldn't be used to predict grades.
