■ WHAT THE NUMBERS TELL YOU

  • Fewer than 1 in 10 marks are for straight recall. Most marks come from applying and explaining.
  • Section B on both papers is mostly AO2 and AO3: you must use the scenario.
  • Learning definitions is the start, not the finish. Practise using them in a context.

Worked example: one question, three answers

Question: A supermarket is replacing half of its staffed tills with self-service checkouts. Discuss the impact of this change on the supermarket and its customers. (6 marks)

Read each answer and decide which level it reaches before you reveal the examiner's comment.

Answer 1: Self-service checkouts are faster and cheaper. But some people don't like them and they can break. Staff could lose their jobs.

Level 1, 2 marks. Three relevant points, but none is explained or linked to the supermarket. Only one side developed. Generic.

Answer 2: The supermarket would save money on wages because fewer cashiers are needed, and queues may be shorter at busy times. However, older customers might struggle with the screens, so they may shop elsewhere. The machines also need maintenance, which costs money.

Level 2, 4 marks. Points are explained with 'because' and 'so', and both sides are considered. Application to the supermarket is there but thin; the impacts on customers are not developed.

Answer 3: Fewer cashiers are needed, so wage costs fall, which the supermarket could pass on as lower prices. Customers buying a few items can pay quickly, cutting queues at lunchtime. On the other hand, customers with low digital skills or disabilities may find the screens difficult, which is a digital inclusion issue; if they feel ignored they may move to a competitor, so keeping some staffed tills matters. 'Unexpected item' errors frustrate customers and still need a member of staff, so savings may be smaller than expected. Theft can also rise when nobody scans the items, increasing losses.

Level 3, 6 marks. Accurate, developed points linked to the supermarket and its customers throughout. Balanced, with clear chains of reasoning. A conclusion isn't required for 'discuss'.

Code questions (Paper 1)

  • Trace, then answer. Draw a trace table in the space provided before writing the output.
  • Indent consistently. Indentation is part of Python's syntax; examiners need to see which lines are inside a loop or if.
  • Small errors still cost marks. Check colons, brackets, == vs =, and converting input() to int or float.
  • Answer what is asked. 'Identify the error' wants the line and the problem. 'Correct the error' wants the fixed line of code.
  • Use meaningful names in any code you write. It makes your logic easier for the examiner to follow and credit.

✗ COMMON MISTAKE: LOSING EASY MARKS

  • Not using the scenario in Section B: generic answers are capped at the lower levels.
  • Writing one long point for a 4-mark 'explain two' question. Separate your two points clearly.
  • Giving a definition when the question asks you to explain an impact.
  • Running out of time on the final extended question, which is often the highest-mark question on the paper.

A4 Information for candidates

T Level exams follow the Joint Council for Qualifications (JCQ) rules for conducting exams. This is a summary written for this guide. Your centre will display the official JCQ notices, and your invigilator's instructions always take priority.

BEFORE THE EXAM

  • Check your timetable: date, time, room and seat number.
  • Arrive at least 15 minutes early. If you arrive late, your work might not be accepted.
  • Bring black pens in a clear, see-through pencil case.
  • Ask about access arrangements well before the exam.

ITEMS NOT ALLOWED

  • Mobile phones, smartwatches, wireless earbuds or headphones, and any device that can store or send information, even if switched off.
  • Notes, revision guides and paper of your own.
  • Correction fluid or tape, erasable pens, highlighters and gel pens in your answers.
  • Hand anything not allowed to the invigilator or leave it where told before you enter.

DURING THE EXAM

  • Don't talk to, signal to or disturb other candidates.
  • Listen to the invigilator and read the instructions on the front of the paper.
  • Write in black ink. Do rough work on the paper and cross out anything you don't want marked.
  • If you feel unwell or need anything, raise your hand.
  • Stay seated until the invigilator says you may leave.

MALPRACTICE

  • Cheating, copying, having a phone on you, or helping others is malpractice.
  • Penalties range from losing marks on the paper to disqualification from the whole qualification.
  • Don't share or discuss the paper's content online after the exam.
  • Don't write offensive material on your paper.

■ IF SOMETHING GOES WRONG

  • Ill on the day, or something serious has affected you: tell the exams office straight away. You may be eligible for special consideration.
  • Fire alarm or disruption: follow the invigilator. Don't talk about the paper while out of the room.
