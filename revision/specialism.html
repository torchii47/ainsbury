BEYOND THE CORE

Occupational Specialism

The occupational specialism is the practical part of the T Level, where you show you can work like a software developer. It builds on everything in the core, so revising the core is also preparing for it. This page maps the practical skills to the guide and gives you projects to practise on.

OS

What to expect

Your teacher will give you the details of how the specialism is assessed. In general you'll work from a client brief and take a piece of software through the whole development process:

  1. Understand the brief: who the client and users are, and what they need.
  2. Plan and design: requirements, algorithms, data and interface.
  3. Build: write clear, robust, well-commented code.
  4. Test: plan the tests, run them, fix what fails and record what you did.
  5. Review: judge how well the solution meets the brief and how you'd improve it.

★ ASK YOUR TEACHER

  • How is the specialism assessed, and when?
  • What evidence do I need to produce (code, documents, screenshots, recordings)?
  • Which tools and languages will be available?
  • Can I see an example brief to practise on?

Practice projects

The best way to prepare is to build small, complete projects from start to finish. Pick one, give yourself a time limit, and work through the checklist below.

ProjectThe client wants...Stretch
Café stock trackerTo record stock levels, warn when an item is running low and save everything to a CSV fileA weekly chart of what's been used, with pandas and matplotlib
